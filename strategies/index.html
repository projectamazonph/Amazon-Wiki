    Advanced Strategies (2026)¶

    The 2026 algorithm changes have shifted the playbook. The old approach — hundreds of exact-match keywords, constant bid micromanagement — is no longer how you scale. Here's what actually works now.

    1. "Engineered Discovery" with Wide Targeting¶

    Amazon's algorithm now evaluates campaigns on three simultaneous layers: eligibility, click-through behavior, and future visibility. That last one is new and it matters — the algorithm is predicting your ad's future performance based on post-click conversion rates.

    What this means in practice: wide targeting is your discovery engine. Broad match, auto targeting, and refined product targeting now do the heavy lifting for finding new intent signals. Your exact-match campaigns become a "foundation" — strictly for brand defense and protecting rankings on proven high-intent terms.

    Target budget split (for a brand with $5K+/month ad spend): - 60% into wide targeting discovery campaigns (broad, auto, product targeting) - 30% into exact-match foundation campaigns (your top 10 revenue keywords) - 10% into Sponsored Brands Video and Sponsored Display retargeting

    2. Single Keyword Ad Groups (SKAGs) for Top Performers¶

    SKAGs are still alive — but they're focused now. Pick your top 10 highest-revenue-driving keywords and give each its own ad group. This gives you clean data and precise bid control on the terms that actually move the needle.

    The rest of your keywords live in broader campaigns where Amazon's algorithm does the matching work.

    3. Margin-Based Campaign Segmentation¶

    Group your ASINs by profit margin profile, not category. A high-margin hero product can tolerate a 25-30% ACoS and still be profitable. A low-margin accessory that breaks even at 15% ACoS cannot. Mixing them in the same campaign means one of them is being mismanaged.

    Segmentation example: - High margin (>40%): separate campaign, ACoS ceiling of 35%, priority budget - Mid margin (20-40%): standard campaign, ACoS ceiling of 25% - Low margin (<20%): tight ACoS of 15%, or consider whether PPC is the right channel

    4. Max Bid Formula from Unit Economics¶

    Stop guessing your bid ceiling. Calculate it:

    Max Bid = Target ACoS × Expected Conversion Rate × Average Selling Price

    Example: Target ACoS = 25%, Conversion rate = 8%, ASP = $45 Max Bid = 0.25 × 0.08 × $45 = $0.90

    This is your break-even bid. Anything below it is profitable. Use placement bid multipliers only after you have 2-3 weeks of data showing that top-of-search converts better than average for your product.

    5. Sponsored Brands Video is Mandatory¶

    In 2026, Sponsored Brands Video is not optional. In competitive niches, static Sponsored Brands ads lose impression share to video. Amazon reports a 9% CTR increase with video over image-only ads. For products with a strong visual story — gadgets, tools, apparel — the ROI is even more pronounced.

    Rule of thumb: if your product needs more than 5 seconds to communicate its value proposition, invest in a 15-30 second video.

    6. AI Creative Agent (New 2026)¶

    Amazon launched an AI Creative Agent that generates multi-format ad creatives within the platform. It's useful for: - Rapid creative variations for A/B testing - Generating headlines and descriptions from product data - Scaling asset production for brands with large catalogs

    The catch: 20-30% of AI outputs still require manual editing or rejection. Don't deploy AI-generated creatives without a human review step.

    Seasonal Playbooks¶

    Q4 Peak / Holiday Season¶

    See the full Q4/Holiday Playbook for the complete guide. Key moves:

    • Start ramping budgets in October, not November
    • Increase bids on your best-converting exact-match terms 2 weeks before Black Friday
    • Use Sponsored Display for cart abandoners starting in November
    • Cap ACoS expectations — Q4 is about revenue velocity, not margin

    Prime Day¶

    See Prime Day Playbook for details. The TL;DR:

    • Prime Day typically drives 2-3x normal sales volume
    • Budget for 3x your normal daily spend
    • Use auto and broad targeting to capture the surge in search流量
    • Consider Sponsored Display deals pages placements for visibility
    • Post-Prime Day: run retargeting for 2 weeks to capture browsers who didn't convert

    Back to School¶

    Relevant for categories like office supplies, electronics, and apparel. See Other Key Dates.

    • Target mid-July through September
    • Keywords shift to "school supplies," "back to school," "college dorm"
    • Sponsored Brands Video works well for parent/decision-maker searches

    Real Campaign Examples¶

    Example 1: New Product Launch (Kitchen Gadget, $35 ASP)¶

    Goal: Build keyword rankings and generate first 50 reviews

    Campaign structure: - Campaign 1: Auto targeting only, $30/day, bids $0.35-0.50 - Campaign 2: 5 exact-match keywords, $20/day, SKAG format - Campaign 3: Competing product ASIN targeting, $15/day

    Results at 30 days: - ACOS: 38% (above target but expected during ramp) - Orders: 47 - Keyword ranking improvement: +12 average rank on primary terms

    Lesson: Auto campaigns during launch generate more data than any other format. Let them run without over-pruning for the first 30 days.

    Example 2: Scaling a Mature Product (Supplement, $45 ASP)¶

    Goal: Lower ACoS from 32% to 22% while maintaining revenue

    Moves made: 1. Identified 3 exact-match keywords driving 60% of spend with ACOS > 40% 2. Moved them to separate SKAG campaigns with tighter bid caps 3. Negated exact-match winners from broad campaigns (stopped self-cannibalization) 4. Increased budget on low-ACOS broad match terms by 40% 5. Added Sponsored Display retargeting at 18% ACoS target

    Results at 60 days: - ACOS: 24% (still above 22% target but trending down) - Revenue: up 8% due to better budget allocation - Key learning: broad match cannibalized exact-match budgets before the negation fix

    Example 3: Seasonal Surge (Gift Category, $60 ASP)¶

    Goal: Maximize revenue during the 3 weeks before Christmas

    Strategy: - Pushed 70% of annual PPC budget into weeks 50-51 - Switched to dynamic bidding-down only (protect against CPC spikes) - Added competitor ASIN targeting for gifting occasions - Used placement bid modifier: +100% on top of search for exact-match SKAGs

    Results: - ACOS: 19% (below 22% target — holiday conversion rate carried it) - Revenue: $48,000 in 21 days (vs. $8,000/month average) - Post-holiday: immediately reduced budget and paused competitor targeting

    ACoS Targets by Product Stage¶

    Stage Typical ACOS Range Notes
    New product / ramp 30-45% Invest to build rank; monitor listing conversion
    Growth 20-30% Balance rank maintenance with profitability
    Mature 15-22% Optimize for margin, use retargeting to protect rank
    Seasonal peak 18-25% Accept higher ACOS for volume
    Decline 25-35% Accept higher ACOS if total profit is positive

    These are benchmarks, not gospel. Your actual target depends on your margin structure and whether you're treating PPC as an investment (building rank) or a profit center (generating margin).
