## What Is Amazon PPC?

Amazon PPC (Pay-Per-Click) advertising lets sellers pay to have their products shown to shoppers on Amazon. You bid on keywords or product targets. When a shopper clicks your ad, you pay. When they buy, you make a profit (if your bids are set correctly).

**The three types of ads you need to know:**

**Sponsored Products** — The most common. Ads appear in Amazon search results and on product detail pages. They promote individual listings. Think of these as your workhorse ads.

**Sponsored Brands** — Your brand logo + headline + up to 3 products. Appears in search results. Good for building brand awareness and driving traffic to your brand storefront.

**Sponsored Display** — Targets shoppers based on their browsing behavior, both on and off Amazon. Works for retargeting people who viewed products (including competitors').

**How the auction works:** When a shopper types a search query, Amazon runs a silent auction among all advertisers targeting that query. Your bid, your listing quality, and your expected click-through rate all factor in. The winner's ad appears. You pay only if the shopper clicks.

Learn more: [What is Amazon PPC](/docs/sections/01-1-foundations-and-fundamentals/1-1-1-1-what-is-amazon-ppc)

## Self-Assessment

Answer these 5 questions honestly. Your score tells you where to focus.

**Q1:** When you pull the search term report, do you know what to do with it?
- (A) I pull it but don't know what I'm looking at → Beginner
- (B) I can identify obvious negatives and winners → Intermediate
- (C) I analyze it weekly and make structured decisions → Advanced

**Q2:** What does ACOS measure?
- (A) I know the formula but not when to use it vs. TACOS → Beginner
- (B) I understand the difference and know when each applies → Intermediate
- (C) I use all three (ACOS, TACOS, ROAS) for different decisions → Advanced

**Q3:** Your campaign has a 45% ACOS on a product with 40% margin. What do you do?
- (A) Lower the bids → Beginner
- (B) Investigate whether the high ACOS is building rank, then decide → Intermediate
- (C) Diagnose first (listing, targeting, structure), then decide based on unit economics → Advanced

**Q4:** How many active campaigns should a new product have in week 1?
- (A) As many as possible — cast a wide net → Beginner
- (B) 1-2 (auto + a few SKAGs), add structure as data proves the need → Intermediate
- (C) It depends on budget and learning goals — I have a framework for deciding → Advanced

**Q5:** What does Smart Bidding require to work properly?
- (A) I use it because it's automatic → Beginner
- (B) 100+ monthly conversions per campaign → Intermediate
- (C) I know the requirements and monitor whether campaigns are meeting them → Advanced

**Scoring:** Count your answers.
- 4-5 (C): You're advanced. Focus on [strategies](/docs/strategies) and [automation](/docs/automation).
- 3-4 (B): You're intermediate. Focus on [tactical optimization](/docs/tips) and [bidding](/docs/sections/06-6-bidding-strategies-and-bid-management).
- 1-2 (B): You're at the intermediate-beginner boundary. Work through Path 2 above.
- Mostly (A): You're a beginner. Start with Path 1 and [this wiki's home page](/docs/sections/00-0-front-matter/0-1-0-1-home-landing-page).

## Quick Wins You Can Implement This Week

These are changes that take under 30 minutes and can measurably improve campaign performance. Do them in order.

### Quick Win 1: Pull Your Search Term Report (15 minutes)

If you've never pulled the search term report from Seller Central, do this today:
1. Seller Central → Campaign Manager → Reports → Search Term Report
2. Set date range to Last 7 days
3. Download CSV
4. Look at the column "7 Day Total Sales" — find any line with spend > $5 and 0 sales
5. Those are your first negatives

This alone can save you money immediately. The search term report is the most underused free tool in Amazon PPC.

### Quick Win 2: Check Your Placement Split (10 minutes)

1. In Campaign Manager, go to any campaign → Placements tab
2. Look at Spend vs. Revenue by placement
3. If product pages are 60%+ of your spend but top of search is <30%, you may be over-indexed on low-converting placements
4. Add a placement modifier: +25% to top of search, -25% to product pages
5. Wait 7 days, then check if ACOS improved

### Quick Win 3: Stop Bidding Against Yourself (10 minutes)

1. Find your exact-match campaigns
2. Find your broad or phrase campaigns
3. Export your exact-match keywords from one
4. Add them as exact-match negatives to the other

Why: If the same keyword is in both campaigns, you're bidding against yourself in the same auction. The negative removes the overlap. Budget flows to one campaign instead of two competing for the same term.

### Quick Win 4: Audit Your Campaign Structure (20 minutes)

Export a list of all your campaigns with spend and orders for the last 30 days. Answer:
- Are any campaigns spending <$50/month? (They likely won't have enough data to learn)
- Are any campaigns at 0% ACOS? (Usually a reporting lag or data sync issue)
- Are ASINs with different margin profiles mixed in the same campaign?

Flag anything that needs restructuring. You don't have to fix it today — just know what needs attention.

### Quick Win 5: Read One Page of This Wiki (15 minutes)

Choose from:
- [What is Amazon PPC](/docs/sections/01-1-foundations-and-fundamentals/1-1-1-1-what-is-amazon-ppc) — if you've never read it
- [Core Metrics Deep Dive](/docs/sections/10-10-metrics-kpis-and-analytics/10-1-10-1-core-metrics-deep-dive) — if you want to understand numbers better
- [Negative Keywords Hub](/docs/sections/09-9-negative-keywords-and-negation-strategy) — if you've never systematically managed negatives

## Case Studies

Real campaigns. Real numbers. Real lessons.

### Case Study 1: The Over-Structured Launch

**What happened:** A new seller created 22 campaigns on day 1 of their product launch. Each campaign had a $10/day budget. After 30 days, 18 campaigns had fewer than 5 total clicks. ACOS across the account was 62%.

**What went wrong:** Over-structured campaigns with underfunded budgets mean Amazon's algorithm never has enough data to learn. $10/day campaigns need 10-15 days just to generate enough impressions for the algorithm to start optimizing.

**What they should have done:** Started with 2 campaigns — 1 auto, 1 exact-match SKAG with their top 5 keywords. Set each at $30/day. Added structure after 30 days of data.

**Lesson:** Simplicity beats complexity when data is thin.

### Case Study 2: The Negative Keyword Neglect

**What happened:** A seller ran an auto campaign for 6 months without adding a single negative keyword. ACOS was consistently 40% on a product with 35% margin. The campaign was losing money.

**What went wrong:** The auto campaign had been feeding irrelevant traffic for months. By month 6, the negative keyword list was essentially empty of relevant terms and full of irrelevant ones. The cost: thousands of dollars in wasted ad spend on search terms that had no chance of converting.

**What they did:** Pulled 6 months of search term data. Found 47 irrelevant search terms with significant spend and zero orders. Added all 47 as exact-match negatives. ACOS dropped to 31% within 3 weeks.

**Lesson:** Auto campaigns need ongoing negative keyword management. Set a weekly reminder and never skip it.

### Case Study 3: The Margin Surprise

**What happened:** A seller had 3 ASINs in one campaign: a flagship product (45% margin), a bundle (38% margin), and an accessory (18% margin). The campaign's blended ACOS was 28%. The seller thought this was fine.

**What went wrong:** The accessory was being advertised at a loss, subsidizing the blended ACOS. Meanwhile, the flagship product — which could have tolerated a 30-35% ACOS and still been profitable — was being under-invested because the accessory's poor performance was dragging down the campaign average.

**What they did:** Split into 3 campaigns by margin profile. Set ACoS targets per campaign based on each product's unit economics. The flagship product's ACOS went to 30% with 40% more spend, generating more rank improvement. The accessory campaign was paused (PPC wasn't viable for it at any reasonable ACOS).

**Lesson:** Always know the margin profile of every ASIN in every campaign.
