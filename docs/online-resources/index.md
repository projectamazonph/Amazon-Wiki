## Blogs & Expert Sites

These are the blogs that regularly publish accurate, actionable Amazon PPC content. Subscribe to the ones relevant to your product category and budget.

### Tier 1 — High Signal, Regular Updates

**[Helium 10 Blog](https://www.helium10.com/blog/)**
- Covers Amazon PPC, keyword research, listing optimization, and product research
- Their blog posts on match types and keyword harvesting are consistently accurate
- Watch: sponsored content is clearly labeled; distinguish between educational posts and product promos

**[JungleScout Blog](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/)**
- Strong on product research and market analysis
- PPC content is solid but sometimes leans toward beginners
- Good for understanding the broader Amazon ecosystem (FBA fees, compliance, etc.)

**[Sellics Blog](https://www.sellics.com/blog/)**
- Data-driven PPC analysis with actual campaign screenshots and metrics
- Their Sponsored Products benchmark reports (published annually) are genuinely useful reference points
- More advanced than most; good for intermediate-to-senior PPC managers

**[Inkwood Digital / Helium 10 Content](https://www.innels.com/blog/)**
- Published the most accurate 2026 Amazon PPC algorithm analysis available
- Practical focus: what changed, what works now, how to adapt
- Subscribe if you want to stay current on platform-level changes

### Tier 2 — Useful but With Caveats

**[Ad Badger](https://www.adbadger.com/blog/)**
- Focused on Amazon PPC tools and campaign optimization
- Their "UnBox 2025" coverage was solid for Amazon Ads updates
- Caveat: tool-centric perspective; some posts read like product promotions

**[AMZ123](https://www.amz123.com/)**
- Chinese Amazon seller community hub; excellent for understanding competitive dynamics in the Chinese seller ecosystem
- Much of the content is in Chinese; use a translation tool if needed
- Useful for cross-border insights and understanding competitor strategies

**[JungleScout's Amazon Seller Podcast Blog](https://www.junglescout.com/blog/amazon-seller-podcast/)**
- Transcripts from their podcast; useful if you prefer reading to listening
- Strong guest interviews with experienced sellers

### Tier 3 — Occasional Gems

Most other Amazon PPC blogs fall into this category. They publish occasionally and the content is hit-or-miss. The Tier 1 and Tier 2 sources above will cover the same ground faster and more accurately.

## YouTube Channels

Video works well for watching someone walk through Seller Central or explain campaign structures visually. These channels are worth your time.

### Worth Subscribing

**Helium 10 YouTube**
- Tutorial-heavy: how to use Helium 10 tools, walkthroughs of campaign setup
- Search "Helium 10 PPC tutorial" for structured learning content
- Good for visual learners who use the Helium 10 suite

**JungleScout YouTube**
- Product research focused but covers PPC strategy in context
- Their "Amazon PPC for Beginners" series is genuinely beginner-friendly

**Selling Family (Dennis O'Neill)**
- Real account management experience, not just theory
- Discusses agency perspective — useful for understanding how professional PPC managers approach campaigns
- Less polished production, more substance

**Mike Zima / DataDive (formerly Zuma Success)**
- Advanced PPC analysis; data-heavy
- Useful if you want to go deeper on attribution and campaign math
- Strong for agencies and advanced sellers

**Andres Marcelo / Ecomcrew**
- Real-world seller experience with honest takes on what works
- Their Amazon PPC content is practical and grounded

### Use With Caution

- Any channel that promises "I'll show you my $50K/day PPC account" without context is selling you something
- Channels where every video starts with "In this video, I'm going to show you 5 tips" are usually padded for SEO
- Channels with no engagement (comments disabled, low view counts on 2+ year old content) are dead — find active creators instead

## Podcasts

Podcasts are great for commutes and workouts. These are worth your listening time.

**The Ecom Podcast**
- Interviews with Amazon sellers and PPC specialists
- Real numbers and honest discussion about failures, not just wins
- Episodes are typically 45-60 minutes; pick the ones relevant to your stage

**The Amazon FBA Podcast**
- Broad coverage: product research, PPC, logistics, scaling
- PPC episodes tend to be more introductory than advanced
- Good for staying aware of what's happening across the Amazon seller ecosystem

**JumpSend (by Helium 10)**
- Data-backed episodes with actual campaign examples
- Frequency is lower but consistency of quality is higher

**The PPC Burrito (Teikametrics)**
- Interview format with agency owners and brand managers
- Useful for understanding how larger brands approach PPC differently than individual sellers

## Forums & Communities

Communities give you access to real-time questions, case studies, and the collective experience of other sellers. The quality varies — learn to distinguish signal from noise.

### Reddit

**r/FulfillmentByAmazon**
- Largest Amazon seller community on Reddit
- Covers PPC, FBA logistics, product research, and account health
- Quality varies widely by post — sort by "Top" and look at posts with 100+ upvotes for quality content
- Good for troubleshooting specific issues ("Why did my campaign ACOS spike after changing bids?")

**r/AmazonSeller**
- Smaller than r/FulfillmentByAmazon but sometimes more focused on PPC specifically
- Less noise than the main subreddit
- Search before posting — many common questions have been answered

**r/PPC**
- Broader PPC community covering Google Ads, Meta, and Amazon
- Useful for understanding cross-platform PPC strategy that applies to Amazon
- Less Amazon-specific content

### Facebook Groups

Search Facebook for:
- "Amazon FBA PPC" groups (typically 5,000-50,000 members)
- "Amazon Seller Mastermind" groups
- Category-specific groups (e.g., "Amazon Supplements PPC" or "Amazon Home Products PPC")

**What to look for in a good Facebook group:**
- Active moderation (spam-free, no constant "DM me for secrets" behavior)
- Detailed posts with actual data (screenshots, numbers)
- Members who ask specific questions rather than vague "how do I lower ACOS?" posts
- Vendors who share value before pitching

**What to avoid:**
- Groups where the top posts are always "I just hit $100K/month — here's my 3-step guide" (affiliate/content farming)
- Groups where every answer is "use [tool name]"

### Discord Servers

Amazon PPC Discord servers exist but are less established than Facebook groups. They tend to be more technical and advanced. Search for servers with active daily message counts if you prefer real-time discussion.

## Official Amazon Resources

**Amazon Advertising API Documentation**
- developer.amazon.com/docs/advertising-api
- The authoritative source for any programmatic Amazon ad management
- Start with the quickstart guide if building integrations

**Amazon Seller Central Help**
- sellercentral.amazon.com/help/hub
- Official documentation for the Campaign Manager interface
- Useful for platform-specific questions (why did my ad stop running? how do I set up bulk operations?)

**Amazon Brand Registry & Brand Dashboard**
- brandregistry.amazon.com
- For brand-registered sellers: access to Brand Analytics, A+ Content, and Sponsored Brands reporting
- Brand Analytics (search frequency rank data) is the most accurate keyword volume data available

**Amazon Ads Blog**
- ads.amazon.com/blog
- Official announcements on new ad formats, policy changes, and platform updates
- Subscribe to the feed — Amazon occasionally changes attribution models or billing without warning

**Amazon Events (UnBox, Accelerate)**
- amazon.com/ads/events
- Annual Amazon Ads conferences with product announcements
- Keynotes and sessions are often posted on YouTube after the event

## Free Courses & Certifications

### Amazon's Own

**Amazon Ads Creative Studio (Free)**
- amazon.com/ads/creative-studio
- Learn to build Sponsored Brands video ads and other creative formats
- Hands-on with Amazon's own creative tools

**Amazon Ads Learning Console (Free)**
- Part of the broader Amazon Ads certification program
- Self-paced courses covering Sponsored Products, Sponsored Brands, Sponsored Display, and DSP basics
- Courses are free; certification exams are free for brand partners

### Platform Courses

**Helium 10 Academy (Free with account)**
- Free courses on keyword research, listing optimization, and PPC basics
- More useful for beginners; advanced content requires paid tools access

**Jungle Scout Academy**
- Free video courses on Amazon selling
- PPC content is introductory level; better for new sellers than experienced managers

**YouTube (crowd-sourced)**
- Search "Amazon PPC course free" and you'll find hundreds of hours of free content
- Quality varies wildly; stick to channels with demonstrated expertise (see YouTube recommendations above)

## Paid Courses — Worth It vs. Skip

### Worth Paying For

**Serious Amazon PPC courses from established instructors — when to pay:**
- You're an agency owner who needs structured training for your team
- You're transitioning into PPC management from a related field (SEO, Google Ads)
- You've taken free resources as far as they can take you and need more

**Look for:**
- Instructor who runs real campaigns (not just a course creator)
- Specific, actionable content (not 3 hours of "here's how PPC works" fluff)
- Community access for Q&A
- Recent content (Amazon's platform changes fast; a course from 2022 may be outdated)

**What to expect to pay:** $200-$2,000 depending on depth. Above $2,000, scrutinize the value carefully — at that price point you're often paying for coaching and community, not just content.

### Skip These

- **"Become an Amazon PPC Expert in 30 Days"** — Any course promising fast mastery of a complex skill is overpromising
- **Courses from instructors who don't run active campaigns** — They teach theory, not practice
- **Courses that spend more than 20% of content on tool-specific tutorials** — The tools change; the principles don't
- **Courses where the main pitch is "join my $10K mastermind"** — The free content is the sales funnel; the mastermind is the product
